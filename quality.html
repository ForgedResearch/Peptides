Research peptide quality and purity testing

How Forged Research documents identity and analytical purity for listed lots — what the methods measure, what the numbers mean, and what the paperwork does not claim.

Our documentation standard

Forged Research supplies lyophilized peptides for in-vitro laboratory work. For each lot we list, we obtain analysis from an independent laboratory and provide a Certificate of Analysis tied to that lot number. We do not run our own analytical lab and we do not describe capabilities we do not have.

Independent third-party analysis

  • RP-HPLC purity — reverse-phase separation with peak-area quantification of the target compound
  • Mass spectrometry identity — observed mass compared with the theoretical mass of the intended sequence
  • Content / quantitation — where the lab reports peptide content per vial for that lot

Ask for the certificate that matches the lot on your vial.

What HPLC purity measures

Purity is the target peak’s area as a share of total detected peak area under that laboratory’s method. It does not identify remaining signal and does not count anything the detector cannot see.

What mass spectrometry measures

Mass spectrometry supports identity by comparing observed mass with the theoretical mass of the intended sequence. Identity and purity are different questions.

What we do not claim

  • We do not hold or claim our own laboratory accreditation
  • We do not claim sterility, endotoxin, bioburden, or heavy-metal testing unless that lot’s certificate reports that test
  • We do not claim GMP manufacturing status
  • We do not treat analytical results as proof of safety, efficacy, or fitness for any purpose
  • We make no medical, therapeutic, diagnostic, or dosing claims — see the FDA Disclaimer

Does high purity mean the material is safe?

No. Purity is a composition measurement under one method. It is not sterility, safety, or regulatory clearance.
